# Rejection Playbook

Use this when the user already has a notice, or a build is stuck. For pre-submission review, use files 1 to 7. Dated rules are in `deadlines.md`.

Evidence levels used below:

- `Google documented`: stated in a Google Play policy or help page that was read.
- `Corroborated`: three or more independent reports. A thread with many comments is one report unless the commenters show separate cases.
- `Anecdotal`: one or two reports, or any vendor or blog claim. Never treat it as a rule.

Reddit was not reachable during research. Field reports come from Hacker News, GitHub issues, builder and engine forums (Unity, Bubble, Adalo, Kodular, App Inventor, RevenueCat), and blogs. Their ranking reflects what was reachable, not how common a failure is. Agency and vendor posts were read but never counted as evidence.

## How to read the notice

Extract these before touching code:

1. **Issue name and policy named.** Copy the exact heading from the email and from Play Console Policy status. Do not paraphrase it in the report.
2. **Affected artifact.** Which version code, track, release, store listing item, or the whole app or account. The Publish page separates app status, update status, and item status, and a rejected item can sit inside an otherwise live app.
3. **Action and state.** Rejection, removal, suspension, limited visibility, limited regions, restricted account, or termination (next section). The state decides what is required.
4. **Deadline.** Any date stated in the notice. For terminations there is a documented 180-day appeal window. For app-level actions no appeal deadline was found in the sources read.
5. **What the email actually says.** Separate three things: the policy it cites, the example it gives, and what it asks you to do. The enforcement page warns that a notice may not list every violation, so the cited one is a floor, not the whole problem.
6. **Appeal link or instructions.** Notices carry them.

Then check the whole app, SDKs, and other apps in the catalog before resubmitting. Third-party code and SDK behavior are the developer's responsibility.

Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234

## Enforcement states

Unless the notice says otherwise, actions affect all regions and stay in effect until an appeal is granted. Do not resubmit a rejected or removed app until every violation is fixed.

| State | App effect | Account effect | What it requires | Appeal |
|-------|-----------|----------------|------------------|--------|
| Rejection (Update rejected, App rejected) | New app or update is not published. For an update, the previously published version stays live. Existing installs, stats, and ratings are untouched | None | Fix and resubmit, or appeal | Link in the email. App rejected applies only to a draft app being published for the first time |
| Removal (Removed by Google) | App and all earlier versions removed; listing hidden; in-app purchases unavailable until a compliant version is approved | Not immediately affected; multiple removals may lead to suspension | A policy-compliant update | Link in the email |
| Suspension (Suspended by Google) | Same as removal; the suspended app's APK or bundle can no longer be used | Counts as a strike; multiple strikes can lead to termination of individual and related accounts | A successful appeal | Link in the email |
| Limited visibility | Discoverability restricted; still reachable by direct link; listing still visible | None | Per the notice | Link in the email |
| Limited regions | Downloadable only in certain regions; earlier installers keep the app but get no updates | None | Per the notice | Link in the email |
| Restricted developer account | All apps removed; cannot publish new or republish; Console still accessible, and you can add information and amend account details | Restricted | Fix all violations, then republish | Link in the email |
| Account termination | All apps removed; related accounts permanently suspended; any new account you open is terminated without refund of the registration fee | Terminated | Appeal only | Within 180 days of the decision for terminations on or after 2026-01-28; if the appeal is filed near the end and denied, 30 extra days to reply with new information |
| Dormant account closure | Console data gone unless reinstated | Closed; registration fee forfeited; Google notifies first; you can open a new account later | Keep the account active | Not stated |

Repeated or serious violations (malware, fraud, user or device harm) lead to termination of individual or related accounts. Rejections do not affect account standing, but repeated rejections or removals can lead to suspension.

An appeal is for a decision made in error, and the policy says to review it carefully first. A limited-visibility or vitals effect (bad behavior thresholds) is not an enforcement action; see file 7.

Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234 , https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859751 , https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16659089

## Framework traps: libraries that add permissions

Libraries and plugins can add permissions through manifest merge, so a rejection can name a permission the code never requested. The rule that ties these together is Google documented: the developer is responsible for third-party code and SDKs. A merge can re-add a permission after a rebuild or dependency bump.

| Adobe AIR with Play Core or Asset Delivery | Foreground service permission on targetSdk 34 | 2024-05 to 2025-02 | Anecdotal | https://github.com/airsdk/Adobe-Runtime-Support/issues/3273 |

| Debug tooling | `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` and similar debug-only permissions leaking into a release manifest | Two repos removed them as prevention; no rejection report | Anecdotal | https://github.com/linagora/twake-drive-mobile/issues/271 |
| Google Mobile Ads and AD_ID | Advertising ID declaration and Data safety agreement | One repo PR and one 2024 forum post; no first-hand rejection wording | Anecdotal | https://community.kodular.io/t/unable-to-update-app-on-playstore/250534 |

Always conclude with this check: **open the merged manifest of the release build** (the AAB you upload, not `app.json`, `AndroidManifest.xml` in source, or the debug build) and list every permission and service. In Android Studio use the Merged Manifest view for the release variant. From a bundle, `bundletool dump manifest --bundle=app-release.aab` should print it (the command is not in the sources read: **Verify** locally). Remove what the app does not use with `tools:node="remove"` or the framework's blocked-permission option, then rebuild and read the merged manifest again.

## Patterns from developer reports

Each pattern below appeared in three or more independent reports. Each is a check to run.

1. **A dependency or no-code builder added a permission you never wrote.** Seen with `READ_MEDIA_*` (Telerik, FishBun, Zendesk SDK, about ten Bubble users, an HN thread), foreground service permissions (expo, Bubble, Adobe AIR, a Flutter location plugin), and `AD_ID` (React Native, a kids app). Check: list the permissions in the merged manifest of the release bundle, name which dependency adds each, and remove or declare it. If the source is a builder, you cannot edit its manifest; ask the builder or wait for its update.
2. **An older build still live on another track keeps the violation alive.** Seen in three Unity threads, the Bubble fix steps, and an Accessibility case where old closed and internal releases still carried the service. Check: open every active track (internal, closed, open, production, and any staged rollout), read the version code and the permissions or engine version of each, and remove or supersede any that still carries the problem. A fix on production alone does not clear it.
3. **Builder platforms lag Play deadlines.** Seen with Adalo (target API and 16 KB, in separate threads), Bubble (media and foreground permissions), and Unity SDK versions. Check: if the app is built by a no-code or engine platform, read the platform's forum for the issue first. The user may only be able to wait for the platform update and then republish a fresh build to all tracks.
4. **Declarations must match what the app visibly does.** Seen with Health Connect scope (a type described only in the permission sheet and not on any screen), health app declarations, and Data safety forms that missed SDK-collected IDs. Check: for each permission or declared data type, find a screen the reviewer would see that uses it, and make the declaration, privacy policy, and in-app text name the same things. The permission-sheet-only case is one report; the broader mismatch pattern is several, some from 2024.

Reports that did not reach three independent cases stay in the wording table as Anecdotal.

## Production access

Documented questionnaire. Applies to personal accounts created after 2023-11-13. Apply from the Dashboard with "Apply for production" after the closed test meets 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days.

1. **About your closed test.** How easy recruiting was; whether testers used all features; whether usage matched expected production behavior, with differences; summary of feedback received and how it was collected.
2. **About your app or game.** Target audience, as specifically as possible; value proposition (for games, what makes it unique); estimated first-year install range. These answers are not shown publicly and do not affect visibility, Console features, or program eligibility.
3. **About your production readiness.** Changes made from what the test taught you; how you decided the app was ready.

Leaving a page without Next or Apply discards the entries. Review usually takes seven days or less, and can take longer. Google may ask you to keep testing.

Does opting out reset the 14 days? Per the testing page, a tester who opts in, tests under 14 days, and opts out does not count. A tester who opts out and later opts back in must have 14 consecutive days to count. The page does not say whether the whole test clock restarts when the number of opted-in testers drops below 12; it says 12 must be opted in continuously for the preceding 14 days when you apply. A vendor says falling below 12 restarts the clock. That claim is Anecdotal and not confirmed.

What field reports add (all Anecdotal):

- An HN poster said 20 testers for 14 days still got a refusal citing engagement, and that Google asked for a restart of the 14 days.
- A hobby app was refused for weak engagement and a very basic app, then passed on a second application after the app was improved and a new test run.
- A testing vendor says to plan another full 14 days after a refusal and that there is no fixed wait. It sells testing, so treat its numbers as marketing.
- No source gave the exact refusal email wording.

Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465

## What does not work

- **Rewording a permission prompt or rationale.** The permission policy tests whether the permission is needed (pickers insufficient), not what the prompt says. Several Bubble users reported that rewording did not clear a photo and video rejection (Anecdotal; consistent with Google documented policy).
- **Resubmitting before every violation is fixed.** The enforcement page says not to, and notices may not list all violations.
- **Fixing only the permission the notice names.** The merged manifest may carry others from the same library.
- **Generic appeals.** Two reports (a nine-app metadata case and a crash suspension) got the same text back after a generic appeal. Appeal with the specific change you made and evidence.
- **Upgrading the Unity editor alone.** Several developers on a patched editor were still flagged because an older bundle stayed live on another track.
- **Removing an SDK call while the SDK stays in.** The SDK can still collect the data or merge the permission (a reported Amplitude case from 2023). Check the final bundle, not your source.
- **Opening a new developer account after termination.** Any new account is terminated too, without refund of the fee. A new account after termination is not a fix.
- **Unofficial account transfers or account purchases.** Prohibited; use the official transfer process. A reported termination after an app transfer should not be generalized.
- **Relying on a past exception.** One thread reported an approved exception later withdrawn (Anecdotal). Re-check each submission.
- **Padding the closed test with passive testers.** The testing page names insufficient engagement as a reason to keep testing.
- **Changing behavior or hiding features during review.** Deceptive Behavior covers modifying or obfuscating behavior during review, and manipulating review is prohibited.
- **Expecting a person to reply.** Developers in two sources said no human was reachable. That is opinion; use the appeal link in the notice.
- **Assuming `blockedPermissions` fixed it.** It was suggested in one issue and not confirmed there. Verify by reading the merged manifest.
- **Assuming `tools:node="remove"` is free.** Removing a permission an SDK needs can break that SDK feature (a messaging SDK lost photo attach). After removing, test the feature that used it.

Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234

## Where reports conflict with Google documentation

- **New account after termination.** An HN commenter and a forum replier treat a new account as the workaround. Google's enforcement page says a new account opened after termination is terminated. Do not advise it.
- **Photo Picker.** One app argues date-filtered gallery matching is core and a picker cannot do it. Google requires pickers unless core functionality cannot be met by them. The appeal was pending, so this is not settled.
- **Foreground service `specialUse`.** Google documents a video for each foreground service type, with `specialUse` taking text. One app used that to avoid a video; reviewers on its PR doubted the fit and no Play result was seen.
- **Unity warning.** A Device and Network Abuse notice for a vulnerable engine reads like a malware policy but is an SDK patch matter. One developer was told by Play support it was cache-related and it cleared alone; most had to patch.
- **Production access.** An HN poster with 20 testers for 14 days was still refused for engagement, which sits uneasily with a numeric threshold. Unresolved.

## Coverage note

No first-hand 2025-26 developer reports were found for App access, account deletion, user generated content, impersonation, loan apps, or spam removals of webview or AI-generated apps. Subscriptions have one price-display case. Reddit could not be read, and Stack Overflow, Medium, and the Play community pages were unreachable or returned no body. Absence here does not mean these failures are rare. Many reports only paraphrase the notice or show a screenshot, and many do not post the outcome.
